Pratt (2025) – Discussion Prep

"On the Evolution of Qualitative Methods in Organizational Research"

🌱 Start here — you don't need to have read the paper

Qualitative research = research that produces findings not arrived at by statistics or numbers. Think interviews, observations, case studies—understanding how and why things happen in context, not just how much.

Pratt's paper traces how this kind of research has evolved in organizational studies over 45 years. Work through the sections below in order—they start simple and build up.

Main themes — click to expand (easy → harder)
1
The three main methods
★ Easy — what are the options?

Organizational researchers use three main qualitative approaches:

  • Case studies (Yin, Eisenhardt): Deep dive into one or a few organizations. Strong on study design.
  • Grounded theory (Glaser & Strauss, Gioia Method): Build theory from data step by step. Strong on analysis.
  • Ethnography (Spradley, Van Maanen): Immersing yourself in the field, observing. Strong on data collection.
2
Qualitative research is having a moment
★ Easy — the big picture

Pratt's paradox: "counts less and counts more"

  • Counts less: Qualitative has moved away from "theme counting" as a quality marker.
  • Counts more: Top journals now have qualitative editors. Many award-winning papers are qualitative. Two of the "most interesting" management papers ever used qualitative data.
3
Tension: standardization vs. creativity
★★ Medium — how do we do it well?
  • Methods like Gioia and Eisenhardt became popular—but risk turning into rigid "cookbook" templates.
  • Pushback: methodological bricolageCombining analytical moves from different methods in service of your research question—not formulaically—mix and match tools to fit your question.
  • Quality comes from fit with your research question, not from following a fixed recipe.
4
How do we judge quality?
★★★ Harder — philosophy of evaluation

Quantitative criteria (validity, reliability, p-values) don't fit qualitative work well.

  • Interpretivist alternatives (Lincoln & Guba): credibility, transferability, dependability, confirmability
  • Newer ideas: convincingness (authenticity, plausibility, criticality), trustworthiness (competence, integrity, benevolence)
  • Quality must be judged within one's ontological and epistemological assumptions—not by positivist standards.
5
Future challenges
★★★ Harder — what's ahead
  • Global research: "Double translation" (informant→theory + language) can distort meaning—violence to experienceSquashing informants' voices under your own theoretical assumptions
  • Open science: Preregistration, data sharing—often impossible or unethical for qualitative work
  • AI: Implications for coding, analysis, writing, and potential fabrication
Discussion questions — think first, then reveal (easy → harder)
★ Easy: What are the three main qualitative methods and what is each one especially good at?
Case studies (study design), grounded theory (analysis), ethnography (data collection). Each has complementary strengths.
★★ Medium: How can we balance methodological rigor with creativity and avoid "cookbook" approaches?
Methodological bricolage—mix tools to fit your RQ. Transparency about choices. Rigor comes from justified decisions, not rigid procedures.
★★ Medium: What does "trustworthiness" look like in practice when we can't use traditional validity/reliability?
Competence (knowing what you're doing), integrity (choices fit together), benevolence (fidelity to data, not "violence to experience"). Show your chain of evidence.
★★★ Harder: How should qualitative researchers respond to open science and replication pressures?
Preregistration and data sharing are often impossible (confidentiality) or unethical. Need qualitative-specific practices; avoid mindless application of positivist standards.
★★★ Harder: What role should AI play in qualitative research (coding, analysis, writing)?
Open question. Consider: transcription, pattern-finding, confidentiality risks, fabrication/detection. Where do methodological decisions stay with the researcher?
