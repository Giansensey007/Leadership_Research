# Pratt (2025) — Step-by-Step Preparation Guide

*You haven't read the paper yet. This guide gets you ready.*

---

## Before You Start

**What is this paper?**  
A review article tracing how qualitative research has evolved in organizational studies over 45 years. It covers *how* we do it and *how* we judge if it's good.

**Time needed:** 45–60 minutes total

---

## Step 1: Get the basics (5 min)

**Goal:** Know what qualitative research is so the paper makes sense.

- [ ] **Qualitative** = words, meanings, "how" and "why" (interviews, observations, case studies)
- [ ] **Quantitative** = numbers, statistics, "how much" (surveys, experiments)
- [ ] **Theme** = a recurring idea you find when coding interview/observation data
- [ ] **Theme counting** = the old (now criticized) practice of judging quality by how many themes you found or how often they appeared

**Quick check:** Can you explain qualitative vs quantitative in one sentence? If yes → next step.

---

## Step 2: Skim the abstract and intro (10 min)

**Goal:** Get the big picture before diving in.

1. Open the PDF and read the **Abstract** (first paragraph).
2. Read the **Introduction** (first 2–3 pages).
3. Don't worry about details. Look for:
   - The "counts less and counts more" idea
   - What two things Pratt focuses on (doing research + evaluating it)
   - The three methods he discusses: case study, grounded theory, ethnography

**Key sentence to find:** *"It counts less by diverging from positivist criteria that equated theme counting as an indicator of research quality... It counts more as interest in qualitative methods has burgeoned."*

---

## Step 3: Learn the three main methods (10 min)

**Goal:** Know what case study, grounded theory, and ethnography are.

| Method | What it is | What it's good at |
|-------|------------|--------------------|
| **Case study** | Deep dive into 1 or few organizations | Study design |
| **Grounded theory** | Theory emerges from data (you don't start with a hypothesis) | Analysis |
| **Ethnography** | Researcher immerses in the field, observes up close | Data collection |

**Names to recognize:** Yin, Eisenhardt (case study); Glaser & Strauss, Gioia (grounded theory); Spradley, Van Maanen (ethnography).

---

## Step 4: Understand the main tension (5 min)

**Goal:** Know the central debate in the paper.

**Template vs creativity**

- **Template** = following a fixed recipe (e.g. Gioia Method, Eisenhardt Method) like a cookbook
- **Risk** = too mechanical, kills real insight
- **Pratt's solution** = *methodological bricolage* — mix and match tools to fit your research question, don't follow one recipe blindly

---

## Step 5: Skim the quality section (10 min)

**Goal:** Know how qualitative research is judged.

- [ ] **Positivist criteria** (validity, reliability, p-values) don't fit qualitative work well
- [ ] **Interpretivist alternatives:** credibility, transferability, dependability, confirmability (Lincoln & Guba)
- [ ] **Newer ideas:** trustworthiness (competence, integrity, benevolence), convincingness
- [ ] Quality must be judged *within* your assumptions, not by quantitative standards

**Skip** the detailed tables if you're short on time.

---

## Step 6: Skim the future section (5 min)

**Goal:** Know the three future challenges.

1. **Global research + language** — "double translation" (informant→theory + language) can distort meaning
2. **Open science** — preregistration, data sharing often impossible or unethical for qualitative work
3. **AI** — implications for coding, transcription, writing, fabrication

---

## Step 7: Prepare your discussion points (10 min)

**Goal:** Have something to say in class.

**Pick 1–2 of these to think about:**

- How can we balance rigor with creativity?
- What does "trustworthiness" mean when we can't use validity/reliability?
- Should qualitative research follow open science practices?
- What role should AI play in qualitative research?

**Optional:** Write down one question you'd like to ask or one point you found interesting.

## Quick reference — terms you'll hear

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Theme counting** | Using number of themes as quality indicator (now criticized) |
| **Methodological bricolage** | Mixing tools from different methods to fit your RQ |
| **Violence to experience** | Squashing informants' voices under your own theory |
| **Double translation** | Informant→theory + language translation; both lose meaning |
| **Template** | Fixed methodological recipe (risk: too mechanical) |
| **Trustworthiness** | Interpretivist alternative to validity |
